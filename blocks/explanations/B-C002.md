  `Φ, Ψ ∈ Eqv(A)`. Then
  `Φ-Sat(A) ∩ Ψ-Sat(A) ⊆ (Φ ∩ Ψ)-Sat(A)`.

## Proof

Recall (`B-D014`) that, for `Θ ∈ Eqv(A)` and `X ⊆ A`, `X` is `Θ`-saturated iff
`X = [X]^Θ`, and `Θ-Sat(A) = {X ∈ Sub(A) | X = [X]^Θ}`. Recall also (`B-C001`)
that `Θ ⊆ Θ'` implies `Θ'-Sat(A) ⊆ Θ-Sat(A)`.

Let `X ∈ Φ-Sat(A) ∩ Ψ-Sat(A)`; in particular `X ⊆ A` and `X = [X]^Φ`. The meet
`Φ ∩ Ψ` is the `S`-sorted equivalence whose `s`-component is the intersection of
the relations `Φ_s` and `Ψ_s`; hence `Φ ∩ Ψ ⊆ Φ` as `S`-sorted relations.

We show `X = [X]^{Φ∩Ψ}`.

- By reflexivity of `Φ ∩ Ψ`, every `x ∈ X_s` is `(Φ ∩ Ψ)_s`-equivalent to
  itself, so `X_s ⊆ [X]^{Φ∩Ψ}_s` for every `s`; that is, `X ⊆ [X]^{Φ∩Ψ}`.
- Conversely, since `Φ ∩ Ψ ⊆ Φ`, every `(Φ ∩ Ψ)_s`-class is contained in the
  corresponding `Φ_s`-class, so `[X]^{Φ∩Ψ}_s ⊆ [X]^Φ_s` for every `s`; that is,
  `[X]^{Φ∩Ψ} ⊆ [X]^Φ = X`.

Therefore `X = [X]^{Φ∩Ψ}`, and so `X ∈ (Φ ∩ Ψ)-Sat(A)`. Since `X` was an
arbitrary element of `Φ-Sat(A) ∩ Ψ-Sat(A)`, we conclude
`Φ-Sat(A) ∩ Ψ-Sat(A) ⊆ (Φ ∩ Ψ)-Sat(A)`. ∎

This is exactly `B-C001` with `Θ = Φ ∩ Ψ` (which refines `Φ`) and the
`Φ`-saturation hypothesis: `(Φ ∩ Ψ)`-Sat(A) is obtained from `Φ`-Sat(A) by
taking the finer relation.

## Notes for the adversarial reader

The argument does not use the `Ψ`-saturation hypothesis at all: the corollary
holds already under the weaker hypothesis `X ∈ Φ-Sat(A)`. This is faithful to
the Lean proof, whose second hypothesis is marked `_hΨ` precisely because it is
unused. The reader is asked to confirm that (i) `Φ ∩ Ψ ⊆ Φ` in the correct
direction (so `[X]^{Φ∩Ψ} ⊆ [X]^Φ`), (ii) the reflexivity step gives the reverse
inclusion, and (iii) dropping `Ψ`-saturation is genuinely harmless rather than a
silently weakened claim.
