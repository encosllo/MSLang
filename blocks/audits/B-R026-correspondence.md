> ## Setting and listed primitives

> The file fixes a universe `u` and works with the following notions (declared
> here with their signatures only; bodies are not shown):

> - `SSet S` — a sorted set over a fixed type `S` of sorts (an `S`-indexed
>   family of types).
> - `Sub A` — a sub-sorted-set of a sorted set `A` (an `S`-indexed family of
>   subsets).
> - `SortedEqv A` — a sorted equivalence relation on `A`.
> - `Signature S` — a signature of sorted operation symbols.
> - `Alg Sig` — a sorted algebra for the signature; `A.1` is its underlying
>   sorted set and `A.2` its algebra structure.
> - `supp A : Set S` — the support of the sorted set `A` (the sorts with

> - `FiniteSSet A : Prop` — `A` is a finite sorted set.
> - `FiniteSub X : Prop` — a sub-sorted-set `X` of a sorted set `B` is finite.
>   From the proof it is used as: `FiniteSub X` unfolds to
>   `Finite (Sigma (fun s => {b : B s // b ∈ X s}))`, i.e. the disjoint union
>   over sorts of the elements lying in `X` is a finite type.
> - `TlGen Sig A` — the inductive family of *translations* of `A`: the smallest
>   class of maps `A.1 t → A.1 s` containing the identities (`refl`), all
>   elementary translations `IsElemTranslation Sig A t s T` (`elem`), and closed
>   under composition (`comp`).
> - `IsElemTranslation Sig A t s T : Prop` — `T` is an elementary (unary derived)
>   translation.
> - `IsFiniteIndex Φ : Prop` — the sorted equivalence `Φ` has finitely many
>   classes.
> - `IsRegularLanguage Sig A L : Prop` — the sub-sorted-set `L` of the algebra
>   `A` is regular. In the proof, after `unfold IsRegularLanguage`, the goal
>   becomes `IsFiniteIndex (congCogenerated Sig A L)`, so this predicate holds
>   exactly when the congruence generated by `L` has finite index.
> - `congCogenerated Sig A L : SortedEqv A.1` — the congruence cogenerated by the

> - `nabla A : SortedEqv A` — the universal (coarsest) sorted equivalence on `A`.

> ## The single theorem
>
> `exists_regular_infinite_language`:
>
> There exist
> - a type `S` of sorts,
> - a signature `Sig : Signature S`,
> - an algebra `A : Alg Sig`,
> - a language `L : Sub A.1` (a sub-sorted-set of the carrier of `A`),
>
> such that all three of the following hold:
>
> 1. `(supp A.1).Finite` — the carrier has only finitely many inhabited sorts;
> 2. `IsRegularLanguage Sig A L` — `L` is regular (equivalently, the congruence
>    it cogenerates has finite index);
> 3. `¬ FiniteSub L` — `L` is *not* a finite sub-sorted-set; i.e. the disjoint
>    union `Σ s, {x : A.1 s // x ∈ L s}` is infinite.
>
> Quantifier structure: an existential over `S, Sig, A, L` followed by a
> conjunction of the three conditions. Note that no finiteness of the individual
> carriers `A s` is assumed or implied — only finiteness of the *support* (the
> set of inhabited sorts). Indeed, the three conditions are jointly satisfiable
> precisely because a sort may have an infinite carrier while contributing just
> one element to the support.
>
> ### Reading of the proof
>
> The witness is the one-sort situation `S = PUnit`, with an algebra whose single
> carrier is infinite (the proof constructs an injection of `ℕ` into it) and the
> language `L` equal to the whole carrier (`fun _ => Set.univ`).
>
> - Support finiteness: the support of the relevant carrier is shown to be all of
>   `Set.univ`, which is finite because `PUnit` has one element.
> - Regularity: the proof shows
>   `congCogenerated r026Sig r026A (fun _ => Set.univ) = nabla r026A.1`, since
>   the language is everything and the congruence it cogenerates is the universal
>   relation. Then `isFiniteIndex_nabla` with the (finite) support gives finite
>   index, which is what `IsRegularLanguage` unfolds to.
> - Non-finiteness: assuming `FiniteSub L`, i.e. `Finite (Σ s, {b // b ∈ L s})`,
>   one embeds `ℕ` by `n ↦ ⟨unit, ⟨n, _⟩⟩`; injectivity yields `Finite ℕ`,
>   contradicting the infinitude of `ℕ`.
>
> ### Mathematical content
>
> For sorted algebras, "regular" (finite index of the cogenerated congruence) is
> conditioned on the support being finite, but does **not** force the language to
> be finite: as soon as some inhabited sort has an infinite carrier, the full
> language on it is regular yet infinite. In particular regularity and finiteness
> of a language are independent once carriers may be infinite, even over a
> one-element set of sorts.
