## Stage 1 -- read-back (fresh agent, Lean only)

> `T_Σ(X)` is the free `Σ`-algebra of finite well-sorted syntax trees.
> `W_Σ(X)` is the algebra of finite words over the alphabet
> `Σ ⊔ X` (same carrier at every sort), with the operation for `f ∈ Σ(w,s)`
> sending rows `r_1,…,r_n` to the row `f r_1⋯r_n`. `T_Σ(X)` is the subalgebra of
> `W_Σ(X)` generated by the one-letter rows `(x)`, and `toT` is the unique
> `Σ`-homomorphism `Term_Σ(X) → T_Σ(X)` sending `var(x) ↦ (x)` and
> `f(t_1,…,t_n) ↦ f toT(t_1)⋯toT(t_n)` (the preorder word reading).
>
> (a) `toT_injective`: at each sort `s`, two terms of sort `s` have the same row
> only if they are the same term — the word reading of a term is unambiguous.
> (b) `term_shape`: every term is a variable, or a nullary operation `f()` with
> `f ∈ Σ((),s)`, or `f(t_1,…,t_n)` with nonempty arity; the alternatives exhaust
> all terms and are pairwise exclusive.

## Stage 2 -- comparison (fresh agent, read-back + contract only)

> **Outcome: `equivalent`.**
>
> The definitions build both `W_Σ(X)` and the generated subalgebra `T_Σ(X)`, and
> `toT` is the unique homomorphism; because `T_Σ(X)` is generated, the image of
> `toT` is all of `T_Σ(X)`, so injectivity makes `toT` a bijection at each sort
> and the term-level shape transfers verbatim to rows. The "if" half of the
> contract (every row of those three forms is a term) is exactly closure of the
> subalgebra under the operations, so the characterization is genuinely on
> `W_Σ(X)_s`, not merely a decomposition of the inductive term type. For the
> crucial case-3 uniqueness, two parses `σ⌢P_1…P_n = σ⌢Q_1…Q_n` lift to terms
> and give `toT(f(t_1,…,t_n)) = toT(f(q_1,…,q_n))`; injectivity forces
> `f(t_1,…,t_n) = f(q_1,…,q_n)`, constructor injectivity gives `t_i = q_i`, hence
> `P_i = Q_i`. Uniqueness of `x`, `σ` and mutual exclusivity follow from
> constructor injectivity and the disjoint alphabet. Thus the declarations prove
> precisely the contract's biconditional and uniqueness.
