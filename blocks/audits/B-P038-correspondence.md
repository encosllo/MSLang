## Stage 1 -- read-back (fresh agent, Lean only)

> # B-P038 — informal read-back
>
> ## Setting
>
> `S` is a fixed type of *sorts*. `SSet S` is the type of `S`-sorted sets (a
> family `s ↦ A s` of types). `Signature S` is a many-sorted algebraic signature.
> `Sub A` is the type of sub-sorted-sets of `A`. `SortedMap A B` is the type of
> sort-preserving maps. `SortedEqv A` is a *sorted equivalence* (an equivalence
> relation on each sort). `Term Sig X` is the inductive type of many-sorted terms
> over the signature with variables from the sorted set `X`.
>
> `sortedEqvLe Φ Ψ` means **Φ refines Ψ** (Φ is the finer relation; as relations
> `Φ ⊆ Ψ`). `sortedEqvInf Φ Ψ` is the binary meet. `IsSat Φ X` means the
> sub-sorted-set `X` is saturated by `Φ`. `sat Φ X` is the saturation of `X`.
> `nabla A` is the universal (coarsest) sorted equivalence on `A`, and `supp A` is
> the support of `A`.
>
> `AlgStruct Sig A` is an algebra structure on `A`; `IsAlgHom` / `IsAlgIso` /
> `IsCongruence` have their usual meanings; `FiniteSSet A` says `A` is finite;
> `IsFiniteIndex Φ` says `Φ` has finitely many classes; `IsAlgHom`,
> `IsCongruence`, etc. are as expected.
>
> `TlGen Sig A t s T` is the inductive closure asserting `T` is a translation:
> identities (`refl`), elementary translations `IsElemTranslation` (`elem`), and
> composition (`comp`). `charEqv L` is the characteristic equivalence of a
> language `L`; `congCogenerated Sig A L` is the congruence cogenerated by `L`.
>
> A *congruence formation* is `G : (A : SSet S) → Set (SortedEqv (Term Sig A))`;
> `IsCongruenceFormation Sig G` says `G` is one.
> `IsFiniteIndexCongruenceFormation Sig G` says `G` is a finite-index congruence
> formation. `congFi Sig F` is the set of finite-index congruences on the algebra
> `F` on `A` (membership carries congruence and finite-index data).
>
> ## Central construction (with body)
>
> `langCongFormationOf Sig L` is defined explicitly by
> ```
> langCongFormationOf Sig L A
>   = { Φ : Φ ∈ congFi Sig (termAlg Sig A).2
>           and ∀ N : Sub (Term Sig A), IsSat Φ N → N ∈ L A }.
> ```
> That is: for each sorted set `A`, collect those **finite-index congruences** `Φ`
> on the term algebra over `A` such that **every language `N` saturated by `Φ`
> belongs to `L A`**. This is the congruence-formation side of the correspondence,
> dual to `langFormationOf` (which sends `G` to `A ↦ { L : ∃ Φ ∈ G A, Φ saturates
> L }`).
>
> ## Theorems with proof bodies
>
> 1. `langCongFormationOf_nabla` (requires `[Finite S]`).
>    If `L` is a regular-language formation and `A` is a sorted set, then
>    `nabla (Term Sig A) ∈ langCongFormationOf Sig L A`.
>    Proof: `nabla` is a congruence (`nabla_isCongruence`), is finite-index
>    (`isFiniteIndex_nabla`, using that `S` is finite so the support of the term
>    algebra is finite), and any `N` saturated by `nabla` lies in `L A` by the
>    regularity of `L`.
>
> 2. `langCongFormationOf_up`.
>    For every `A`: if `Φ ∈ langCongFormationOf Sig L A` and `Ψ` is a congruence
>    on the term algebra over `A` with `Φ ≤ Ψ` (Ψ coarser), then
>    `Ψ ∈ langCongFormationOf Sig L A`.
>    Proof: `Ψ` is a congruence by hypothesis, finite-index by
>    `IsFiniteIndex_of_le` (coarsening a finite-index relation stays finite-index),
>    and saturation is antitone (`sat_antitone`): any `N` saturated by `Ψ` is
>    saturated by `Φ`, hence lies in `L A`.
>    So each `langCongFormationOf Sig L A` is upward closed under coarsening.
>
> 3. `langCongFormationOf_inf`.
>    If `L` is a regular-language formation and `Φ, Ψ ∈ langCongFormationOf Sig L
>    A`, then `sortedEqvInf Φ Ψ ∈ langCongFormationOf Sig L A`.
>    Proof sketch: the meet is a congruence (`IsCongruence_inf`) and finite-index
>    (`IsFiniteIndex_inf`). Given `N` saturated by the meet, `N` is decomposed as
>    a finite union of "atoms" (`eq_iUnion_atoms`, `atomOf`); each atom lies in
>    `L A` because it is the intersection `atomOf Φ ∩ atomOf Ψ`
>    (`atomOf_inf`), and each of those lies in `L A` by the two membership
>    hypotheses, using that `L` is closed under finite unions
>    (`regularFormation_iUnion_finite`) and intersections
>    (`regularFormation_inter`). Hence `N ∈ L A`.
>    So each `langCongFormationOf Sig L A` is closed under binary meets.
>
> 4. `langCongFormationOf_ker`.
>    Hypotheses: `L` is a regular-language formation; `Θ` is a congruence on
>    `Term Sig B`; `Θ ∈ langCongFormationOf Sig L B`; `f : Term A → Term B` is an
>    algebra homomorphism such that the composite of `f` with the projection
>    `prAlg ... Θ ...` is surjective in every sort.
>    Conclusion:
>    `ker (fun s => prAlg Sig (termAlg Sig B).2 Θ hΘ s ∘ f s) ∈
>    langCongFormationOf Sig L A`.
>    Proof: set `g` to be that composite; it is an algebra homomorphism
>    (`isAlgHom_prAlg`); its kernel is a congruence (`ker_isCongruence`); the
>    induced map `Term A / ker g → Term B / Θ` is an algebra isomorphism
>    (`quotAlg_ker_isAlgIso`, using surjectivity). This transfers finite-indexness
>    of `Θ` to `ker g` (`finiteSSet_of_isAlgIso`, `isAlgIso_symm`). Finally, given
>    `N` saturated by `ker g`, the language `sat Θ (directImage f N)` lies in
>    `L B` (via `Θ`'s membership), and the surjectivity hypothesis yields that
>    `N` is saturated by the kernel of the appropriate composite, so a final
>    application of `langFormationOf`-style closure (the "ker" field of the
>    regularity of `L`) puts `N ∈ L A`. Hence the kernel lies in
>    `langCongFormationOf Sig L A`.
>    This is closure under kernels/inverse images along surjective homomorphisms.
>
> 5. `langCongFormationOf_isFiniteIndexCongruenceFormation` (requires `[Finite S]`).
>    If `L` is a regular-language formation, then `langCongFormationOf Sig L` is a
>    finite-index congruence formation.
>    Proof assembles the four expected closure fields:
>    - contains `nabla (Term A)` (`langCongFormationOf_nabla`), and every member is
>      a congruence and finite-index by construction;
>    - closed under binary meet (`langCongFormationOf_inf`);
>    - upward closed under coarsening by congruences (`langCongFormationOf_up`);
>    - closed under kernels of the relevant surjective homomorphisms
>      (`langCongFormationOf_ker`).
>    Together with the observation that each member is (by definition) a
>    congruence with finite index.
>
> ## Declarations (bodies not given)
>
> ### Core order, quotient, saturation
> - `sortedEqvLe Φ Ψ`: refinement order (Φ finer than Ψ).
> - `sortedEqvInf Φ Ψ`: binary meet.
> - `sat Φ X`: saturation of `X` by `Φ`; `sat_antitone`: if `Φ ≤ Ψ` and `Ψ`
>   saturates `X`, then `Φ` saturates `X`; `sat_idem`: `sat Φ (sat Φ X) = sat Φ X`.
> - `IsSat Φ X`: `X` is saturated by `Φ`.
> - `isSat_iff_sortedEqvLe_charEqv`: `Φ` saturates `L` iff `Φ ≤ charEqv L`.
> - `charEqv L`: characteristic equivalence of `L`.
> - `pr Φ s`: projection `A s → Quotient (Φ s)`.
> - `quot Φ`: quotient sorted set; `quotAlg Sig F Φ hΦ`: quotient algebra.
> - `prAlg`: projection homomorphism; `isAlgHom_prAlg`: it is a homomorphism.
> - `quotLe Φ Ψ h` (for `h : Φ ≤ Ψ`): canonical map `quot Φ → quot Ψ`;
>   `quotLe_mk`: it sends the `Φ`-class of `a` to the `Ψ`-class of `a`.
> - `quotLift Φ f h` (for `h : Φ ≤ ker f`): `f` factored through `quot Φ`.
> - `quotAlgLift_isAlgHom`: the lifted map is an algebra homomorphism.
> - `quotAlg_ker_isAlgIso`: **first isomorphism theorem**: a surjective algebra
>   homomorphism induces an isomorphism `A / ker f ≅ B`.
> - `quotOp Sig F Φ p σ`: operation induced on the quotient; `quotOp_mk`: on
>   representatives `quotOp ... (fun i => mk (a i)) = mk (F p σ a)`.
>
> ### Algebra maps and finiteness
> - `IsAlgIso Sig FA FB f`: `f` is an algebra isomorphism.
> - `isAlgIso_symm`: the inverse of an algebra isomorphism is an algebra
>   isomorphism.
> - `finiteSSet_of_isAlgIso`: finiteness transfers along an algebra isomorphism.
> - `directImage f X`: image of a sub-sorted-set under a sorted map.
> - `isFiniteIndex_nabla`: if `supp A` is finite then `nabla A` is finite-index.
>
> ### Congruences and kernels
> - `IsCongruence_inf`: the meet of two congruences is a congruence.
> - `IsFiniteIndex_inf`: the meet of two finite-index sorted equivalences is
>   finite-index.
> - `IsFiniteIndex_of_le`: if `Φ ≤ Ψ` and `Φ` is finite-index, then `Ψ` is
>   finite-index.
> - `closesUnderEtl_of_closesUnderTl`, `closesUnderEtl_of_isCongruence`,
>   `closesUnderTl_of_closesUnderEtl`, `isCongruence_iff_closesUnderEtl`:
>   the equivalences between congruence, Etl-closure, and Tl-closure described in
>   the names.
> - `IsElemTranslation`: elementary translation predicate.
> - `congCogenerated Sig A L`, `congCogenerated_isCongruence`,
>   `congCogenerated_le_charEqv`: the cogenerated congruence, that it is a
>   congruence, and that it refines `charEqv`.
> - `isSat_iff_le_congCogenerated`: for a congruence `Φ`, `Φ` saturates `L` iff
>   `Φ ≤ congCogenerated Sig A L`.
> - `le_congCogenerated_of_isCongruence`: if `Φ` is a congruence and
>   `Φ ≤ charEqv L` then `Φ ≤ congCogenerated Sig A L`.
> - `ker f`, `ker_isCongruence`: the kernel and that it is a congruence for
>   algebra homomorphisms.
> - `nabla_isCongruence`: `nabla A` is a congruence.
>
> ### Languages and formations

> - `IsRegularLanguageFormation Sig L`: `L` is a regular-language formation.
> - `RegularLanguages Sig A` (`regularLanguages`): the set of regular languages of
